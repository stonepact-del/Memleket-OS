# Fictional phone OS experience

## Approved presentation

On mobile, the page itself is the fictional phone. Do not place the experience inside a physical iPhone bezel, handset render, or decorative device mockup. On desktop, present the same apps and shared life state through a coherent fictional computer workspace. The desktop presentation must remain personal and app-centric, never an operations dashboard.

The core OS experience includes:

- a lock screen with time, date, relevant notification previews, and an intentional unlock action;
- a home screen with app icons, useful widgets, badges, and a fixed dock;
- an OS notification surface that leads to the responsible app;
- consistent system navigation and app opening/closing behaviour;
- app-native screens whose information hierarchy matches their task;
- responsive, accessible controls that remain usable at narrow phone widths.

## Quality bar

Aim for premium role-play-phone quality: deliberate typography and spacing, tactile but restrained motion, polished empty/error/loading states, meaningful app transitions, strong touch targets, and convincing information density. “Premium” means coherent and carefully authored, not photorealistic chrome or ornamental complexity.

The design must be original. Do not clone Apple or Android trade dress, FiveM resources, commercial role-play phone scripts, their layouts, icon arrangements, copy, or branded interaction patterns. Familiar OS conventions may support usability, but MemleketOS needs its own visual system and voice.

## Consistency with identity

OS consistency and individual app identity must coexist. Status treatment, system navigation, accessibility, motion principles, notification behaviour, and core tokens should feel shared. Within that frame, each app should use task-appropriate composition, density, navigation, data visualization, and interaction patterns. See [APP_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](./APP_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md) for the app directions.

Do not solve consistency by making every app a stack of identical rounded cards. Conversely, do not make app identity so strong that navigation, terminology, or basic controls cease to behave like one operating system. Every visible interactive control must have a working outcome; unfinished ideas should remain out of player-facing surfaces.
